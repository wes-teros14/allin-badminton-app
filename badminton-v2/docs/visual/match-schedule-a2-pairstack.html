Pair stacks

Theme

Two faces overlapping become one team token, at every level of the list — 34 px live, 26 px next, 20 px later. The overlap costs about 30 % less width than two separate circles, which is what makes it affordable all the way down to the one-line rows.

Match schedule

Why this one

  • The partnership is the unit, and it looks like one. Badminton here is doubles — pairing is the thing the app is built around, right down to the Partners leaderboard. Two overlapping faces say “these two, together” better than two names joined by an ampersand.
  • Faces survive all the way down the list. Scroll to game 19 and you can still spot yourself, which A1 gives up on.
  • Cheaper than it looks. The overlap means a duo occupies about 1.7 circles of width, so the one-line rows stay one line.

Trade-offs

  • 20 px is below the size a face reads at. On the Later rows the stacks work as colour-coded blobs — pattern recognition, not recognition. Honest about it: they help you find a repeat pairing, not identify a stranger.
  • The 2 px ring has to match whatever is behind it, so a stack on a card and a stack on the page ground need different ring colours. Two variants to maintain.
  • It slightly flattens the weight difference that Option A is built on, because now every level has an image.

The fallback problem — worth deciding

Real issue Avatar.tsx falls back to bg-muted with text-muted-foreground and a single initial. Grey-on-grey is low contrast at any size, and every Ana, Ate and Alex collapses to the same “A”. Stacking makes this worse: two grey circles overlapping look like one smudge.
