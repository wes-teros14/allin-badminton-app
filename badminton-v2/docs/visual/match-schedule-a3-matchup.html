Matchup band

Theme

Faces lead. The live card becomes a 2-versus-2 band — four 48 px portraits across with names under them and the net down the middle. It reads like a fixture graphic rather than a list row, and it is the only one where you find your court by looking rather than reading.

Match schedule

Why this one

  • 48 px is comfortably above the recognition threshold. These are the only faces in any of the three options big enough to identify someone you have played once.
  • The layout states the shape of the game. Two left, two right, a net between — anyone who plays doubles reads it instantly, with no “vs” label needed.
  • It suits the moment. You look at the live card when you are walking to a court and want to know who you are up against. That is a glance, not a read.
  • Names sit under the faces, so long nicknames wrap instead of truncating — no more Alexis (Cru….

Trade-offs

  • The live card is roughly 40 % taller than in A1 or A2. With two courts running, the two hero cards nearly fill the first screen and Up next drops below the fold.
  • Four fixed-width name columns at 384 px means about 64 px each. Fine for “Boyet”, tight for anything longer.
  • It commits hardest to avatars, so it is the option that suffers most when a player has no photo — a 48 px grey letter circle is very visible.
  • Up next and Later fall back to a small 4-up strip, so the treatment is not really consistent down the list. A2 is the consistent one.

The fallback problem — worth deciding

Real issue Avatar.tsx falls back to bg-muted with text-muted-foreground and a single initial — low contrast, and every Ana, Ate and Alex collapses to “A”. This option magnifies it more than the other two, because the faces are the layout.
