Status — the fix described here was NOT shipped. The arithmetic on this page is correct and still worth reading: on 2 courts a queue gap of 3 really is zero rest. But the remedy it proposes — scaling the general rest target by court count (idealGap = (idealRestGames + 1) × courtCount) — was built, measured and then reverted. The general rest target is court-blind, exactly as before.

What shipped instead is the First-on-court rule: players in games 1..courtCount are penalised for reappearing at or below courtCount × 2, graded so a wider gap costs less. See tasks/todo.md for the shipped design.

1 court

Aian: Game 1 → Game 4

  1. gap4 − 1 = 3
  2. rounds later (gap ÷ courts)3 ÷ 1 = 3
  3. rounds off (− 1)3 − 1 = 2
  4. minutes (× 20)40 min
40 min — exact, no averaging needed

Why “− 1”? Because gap ÷ courts tells you how many rounds later your next game is, and the very next round isn't a rest — you're playing in it. One round later = zero rounds off.

The half-game caveat. On 2 courts the sum gives 0.5 rounds, but the real answer swings between 0 and 1 full game depending on how far apart the two courts are running. At the opening both courts start on the same second, so the swing is nearly zero and Aian genuinely gets nothing. Later in the night the courts have drifted several minutes apart and the same gap of 3 hands him that drift as a real breather. The formula is the average; the opening is the worst end of it.

4The opening bonus penalty — 2+ courts only

Corrected: at 1 court there is no opening window at all.

The extra penalty at the start exists for exactly one reason: the courts begin perfectly in step. Both start at 7:00, so a short gap there is guaranteed to give nothing. By game 9 they have drifted apart and the same gap buys real minutes.

What this page said before was wrong. It claimed the window is 2 × courts for any court count, so 1 court → games 1–2, and that this “becomes” the Max Consecutive Games setting. It does not. Max Consecutive Games applies to all 14 games; a games-1–2 window applies to two of them. They are not the same rule.

The correct version: a single court cannot drift out of step with itself, so there is nothing special about its opening. The window should simply not exist below 2 courts.

opening window  =  2 × courts,  only when courts ≥ 2

At 1 court a short gap is a short gap wherever it happens, and the ordinary penalty already covers it. Nothing extra is needed, and nothing regresses.

Honest caveat: 2 rounds is a judgement call, not a derived truth. The courts don't snap out of step at round 3 — they drift gradually. 2 rounds is a reasonable cut, and it should be a tunable setting rather than a hard-coded constant.

5One place 1 court and 2 courts genuinely differ

At 1 court the opening could be perfect. At 2 courts it can't be.

Seats in the first 2 roundsPlayers availableCan everyone be different?
1 court (games 1–2)2 × 4 = 814 Yes, easily
2 courts (games 1–4)4 × 4 = 1614 No — 2 must repeat

On 2 courts with 14 people, two repeats in games 1–4 are forced by arithmetic. So the opening rule must be a penalty that shrinks as the gap widens, never a flat ban. Otherwise the app has no reason to prefer Game 1 → Game 4 (the widest option, and what it already chose) over Game 1 → Game 3 (the worst one) — and you could end up with something worse than tonight.
