What it fixes

  • Court number appears at all. Today the list never says which court a game is on — the court strip lives on the other schedule view. “Where do I go” is the first question a player has.
  • Three row weights instead of twenty identical cards. Live is a full card, next is a medium row, later is one line. You can find “what’s happening” without reading anything.
  • Played games are folded away, not dimmed. Today they keep full-size cards at opacity-50, so after 15 games three quarters of the scroll is greyed history — and 50 % opacity on body text drops it under AA.
  • Results are shown. match_results.winning_pair_index already exists; a ✓ that only says “finished” throws it away.
  • Progress meter answers “how far in are we” in one glance.

Trade-offs

  • Game order is broken up. If you think in game numbers (“what’s game 14?”) you now scan a section rather than one continuous list. Option B keeps the order intact.
  • Three row templates is three things to keep consistent as the app changes.
  • “2 games away” is a count, not a time. Honest, but less useful than minutes — and minutes would have to be estimated from average game length, which is a guess.
