Status — the fix described here was NOT shipped. The arithmetic on this page is correct and still worth reading: on 2 courts a queue gap of 3 really is zero rest. But the remedy it proposes — scaling the general rest target by court count (idealGap = (idealRestGames + 1) × courtCount) — was built, measured and then reverted. The general rest target is court-blind, exactly as before.

What shipped instead is the First-on-court rule: players in games 1..courtCount are penalised for reappearing at or below courtCount × 2, graded so a wider gap costs less. See tasks/todo.md for the shipped design.

Why Game 1 → Game 4 is no rest at all

Aian & Sim played Game 1, then Game 4. On paper that is three games apart. On the clock it is zero minutes. Here is why, and what to do about it.

1Watch the clock, not the list

Two courts run at the same time. So the game numbers move twice as fast as the clock does.

7:00
Game 1 starts Court 1 Aian & Sim vs Luis & Ronwald
7:00
Game 2 starts Court 2 At the exact same second — that is what two courts means.
7:20
Both games end Aian & Sim walk off Court 1.
7:20
Game 3 starts Court 1 The court that just freed up takes the next game in the queue.
7:20
Game 4 starts Court 2 Aian & Sim are in this one. They have been off court for 0 minutes.
They finished Game 1 at 7:20 and started Game 4 at 7:20. They walked across to the other court and played again. Three games apart on the sheet. No break at all in real life.

2The rule of thumb

With two courts, halve the gap, then take one off. That is your real rest.

real rest  =  gap ÷ 2 − 1
ExampleGapReal restWhat it feels like

One wrinkle: a gap of 3 is a gamble. Its rest is however long the other court takes to finish. If both games end together you get nothing. If the other court runs 6 minutes long, you get 6 minutes. A gap of 4 is a guarantee.

3The app is aiming at the wrong number

It wants a gap of 3. A gap of 3 is exactly the bad case.

The setting is called “Ideal Rest Between Games: 2”, and the app turns that into 2 + 1 = 3 — meaning “leave 3 places in the list.” It never asks how many courts there are. So:

Aian & Sim at a gap of 3 hit the target exactly. The app charged them no penalty and paid them a +300 bonus for good rest. It was not tolerating that pairing. It was rewarding it.

The whole fix is one multiplication:

target gap  =  (ideal rest + 1) × number of courts

On 2 courts that moves the target from a gap of 3 to a gap of 6. The same pairing goes from +300 bonus to −90 penalty — a 390-point swing, from one change.

4What is actually possible with 14 players

14 players, 14 games, 4 games each. Not everything can be fixed — here is the honest limit.

Nobody has to play back-to-back. A schedule exists where all 14 players get at least a gap of 3 every time. So Aian & Sim's night was avoidable — it was a choice the app made.
But not everyone can get a real sit-down break. About 6 of the 14 will end up with one short gap somewhere. That is arithmetic, not a bug.

Why 6? For a player to get a real break every single time, their four games have to be spread wide — roughly games 1, 5, 9 and 13:

5So why did only Aian & Sim stand out?

Six or more players probably had the same short gap today. You only noticed one.

Because they were partners both times. Two names moved together, so it read as “they never left the court.” If Kurt had a gap of 3 with David, and then another with Steph, nobody blinks — it looks like two unrelated games. “Aian & Sim… Aian & Sim” jumps off the screen.

And the app barely cares: playing with the same partner twice costs 150, against the +300 it was paying for the gap. The repeat partnership was profitable.

6The fix, in five steps

Nothing here has been built yet.

  1. Tell the generator how many courts there are. It genuinely does not know. The number is already sitting on the screen next to it — it just never gets handed over.
  2. Ban the two worst cases outright. Gap of 1 (on two courts at once — impossible) and gap of 2 (finish and restart on the same court). Both are avoidable at your session size, so forbid them rather than discourage them.
  3. Double the rest target. “Ideal rest 2” should mean a gap of 6, not 3. And set the default to 1, because 2 full games of rest is more than a 14-game night can give anyone.
  4. Make the short gap actually cost something — extra if it is the same pair. Today a short gap costs 30 points, while an uneven match costs 300. Rest loses every argument it enters. Raise it to around 150, and add a surcharge when the same two people are together both times — that is the version you actually notice.
  5. Show the best possible score next to the real one. “6 of 14 players had a short gap — 6 is the minimum possible.” Without that second number there is no way to tell a good schedule from a bad one, which is exactly where the screenshot left you.
